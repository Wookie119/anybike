What does CVO really mean?

CVO is Harley-Davidson's Custom Vehicle Operations programme, established in 1999 to offer limited-volume, high-specification factory-custom motorcycles. It is not one motorcycle model or an independent custom shop. Harley-Davidson says the original FXR2 and FXR3 were each limited to 900 examples.

The factory has used CVO for special paint, finishes and equipment, as well as engines and technology sometimes introduced on CVO editions before wider use. The name Screamin' Eagle was used for many earlier editions; Harley-Davidson says CVO branding replaced that naming convention from 2009.

How CVO developed

1999–2000 · FXR2, FXR3 and FXR4

Harley-Davidson revived the respected FXR platform for the first CVO editions. The 1999 FXR2 and FXR3 were factory customs with restricted production; the FXR4 followed in 2000. Check the exact model code and original paint before describing a bike as an authentic early CVO.

2000s · Screamin' Eagle touring and cruiser models

Special-edition Road Glides, Road Kings, Electra Glides and Softails broadened CVO beyond the FXR. Paint, factory accessories and engine upgrades changed by model year, so a single generic CVO technical specification would be misleading.

2010s · Premium touring technology

CVO editions helped introduce larger-displacement factory engines and high-end touring equipment. Some innovations later appeared on regular Harley-Davidson models.

2020s · Modern touring and adventure

Factory CVO Road Glide and Street Glide variants remain distinct from their standard counterparts. The CVO Pan America also extended the programme to the Revolution Max adventure family.

Why collectors value CVO — and what to check

Desirability depends on the specific edition, production year, originality, documented provenance, mileage and condition. A custom-painted or accessorised Harley-Davidson is not automatically a CVO.

  • Check VIN, factory model code and original model-year documentation.
  • Compare factory paint and trim with the correct edition, including replacement panels and accessories.
  • Confirm original engine and equipment; not every CVO shares the same displacement or specifications.
  • Inspect condition, service history, recalls, keys, original parts and any non-factory work.
  • For export, confirm destination-country rules and original registration documentation before purchase.

Factory CVO versus independent Harley customs

A CVO is produced by Harley-Davidson. Independently commissioned builds from specialists such as Arlen Ness or Battistinis may be more extensively customised, but they should be identified by their builder and actual base motorcycle, not described as factory CVO unless verified.

Screamin’ Eagle, S&S Cycle and CVO: what is the difference?

Screamin’ Eagle is Harley-Davidson's performance-parts name and was used for many early factory CVO editions. CVO identifies a limited-production, premium factory motorcycle programme. S&S Cycle is a separate performance engine and components manufacturer founded in 1958, with deep connections to Harley-Davidson racing and custom builds.

A standard Harley upgraded with Screamin’ Eagle parts is not necessarily a factory CVO. A custom Harley with an S&S engine is not necessarily a factory CVO either. Confirm the original Harley-Davidson model code, VIN, build year, factory paint and supporting records before claiming an edition is genuine.
