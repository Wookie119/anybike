Adventure · Transmission comparison

BMW R 1300 GS Adventure: manual or ASA?

BMW's Automated Shift Assistant can operate the clutch and change gears automatically on equipped bikes. Unlike a conventional manual, riders need not work a clutch lever when setting off or stopping; gear selection can still be controlled by the rider. The choice matters most for slow-speed manoeuvres, commuting and extended travel.

Sport motorcycles · Performance comparison

Superbike or sport tourer?

The Ducati Panigale V4 and Kawasaki Ninja H2 SX SE each pursue serious performance differently. Ducati's Panigale development focuses on aerodynamics, cornering control and track ergonomics; Kawasaki adds touring comfort and rider-assistance equipment to a supercharged engine. Comparing them meaningfully means looking beyond horsepower to riding position, equipment and intended journeys.

Japan · Motorcycle history

Honda Super Cub: the small motorcycle with a global story

Launched in 1958, the Super Cub was designed around ease of use and practical everyday transport rather than outright performance. Its step-through layout, semi-automatic transmission and approachable proportions helped it find roles in commuting, delivery work and family transport worldwide.

Honda announced cumulative production of 100 million Super Cub motorcycles in 2017, illustrating how widely a simple design can travel.

India · Heritage & adventure

Royal Enfield: from the Bullet to the Himalayan

Royal Enfield's roots stretch back to Britain in 1901, but its continuing story is deeply connected to India. Bullet production developed in Chennai after the 1950s partnership with Madras Motors. The later Himalayan shifted attention towards an adventure motorcycle intended for varied road surfaces and longer journeys.

It's an example of how a manufacturer's history and the riding environment of a country can influence its motorcycles.

Italy · Industrial design

Vespa: how practical transport became a design icon

The original Vespa emerged in 1946 as Italy looked for accessible post-war transport. Its protective legshield, step-through form and distinctive bodywork made it instantly recognisable. Across generations, the scooter has retained a strong visual identity even as its engines, brakes and electronics have changed.

The Vespa story shows that motorcycle and scooter design can be about convenience and culture as much as raw performance.

Why the same motorcycle means different things in different markets

A lightweight commuter may be someone's daily work vehicle, while the same class of motorcycle may be a leisure purchase elsewhere. Road conditions, passenger needs, fuel availability, servicing networks and local licensing rules shape what riders value.

Honda's account of the Super Cub's international development illustrates why manufacturers adapt models to local needs. This also explains why international comparisons should avoid assuming that every market wants the same specification.

Design & engineering

Why motorcycles feel different

A sports motorcycle places greater emphasis on aerodynamic bodywork, strong braking and a committed riding position. Touring machines generally prioritise long-distance comfort, wind protection and carrying capacity. Engine layout, chassis geometry and weight distribution all contribute to how a bike behaves.

Technology & riding

Automatic or manual?

Most conventional motorcycles use a rider-operated clutch and gear lever. Scooters often use continuously variable transmissions, while some motorcycles offer dual-clutch or automated-shift systems. These technologies differ in how they change gear and how much control the rider retains.

International perspectives

One bike, different countries

A motorcycle suited to open-road touring in one country may be used for city commuting or rural travel elsewhere. Climate, road surfaces, infrastructure, licensing rules and parts availability shape how people choose and use motorcycles around the world.

What makes a useful motorcycle comparison?

Engine capacity alone rarely tells the whole story. An informative comparison considers the correct production years and variants, riding position, fuel capacity, weight measured on a consistent basis, available electronic equipment, transmission choices and the jobs each motorcycle is designed to do.

Where real riders and reviewers disagree, their impressions should be credited as opinions rather than presented as technical facts. Different riding impressions should be attributed to their original reviewers.
