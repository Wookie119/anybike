Indian Motorcycles

A motorcycle name born in 1901, a celebrated Scout and Chief, a factory closure, decades of changing ownership and an extraordinary return. The story of Indian is not one uninterrupted company — it is a remarkable marque with distinct eras.

From Springfield to a modern American marque

1901

The beginning

George Hendee and Oscar Hedstrom helped establish the Indian name in Springfield, Massachusetts. Indian became one of America's pioneering motorcycle manufacturers.

1910s–1940s

Engineering, racing and icons

Indian built a reputation in competition and produced celebrated motorcycles including the Scout, Chief and Indian Four. Exact specifications differ by production year and generation.

1953

The original factory era ends

The original Springfield-based motorcycle manufacturing operation ceased. The Indian name survived through later owners and attempted revivals; those machines must not be mistaken for a continuous Springfield production line.

1998–2011

Victory and Polaris

Polaris launched Victory motorcycles in 1998, building experience with American V-twin motorcycles. Polaris acquired the Indian Motorcycle business in 2011. Victory remains a separate motorcycle brand with its own history.

2013 onward

A modern rebirth

Polaris introduced a new generation of Indian motorcycles, including the Thunder Stroke-powered Chief family. Modern Scouts and Chiefs draw on historic names but are mechanically distinct from the originals.

Ownership, trademark rights, manufacturing continuity and model names are different historical questions. AnyBike keeps the eras separate rather than merging motorcycles solely because their names match.

Motorcycles that made the Indian name

Scout · introduced 1920

The lightweight performance legend

The original Scout became a celebrated Indian model family and the basis of Burt Munro's record-setting motorcycle. The modern Scout uses a different engine, chassis and engineering lineage.

Chief · introduced 1922

American touring heritage

The Chief name spans multiple Indian eras, including classic side-valve machines and modern V-twin motorcycles. Each generation needs its own factory documentation, not one interchangeable specification.

Indian Four · 1920s–1940s

A four-cylinder chapter

Indian's four-cylinder touring motorcycles form a distinct part of the marque's history. They must not be confused with the modern V-twin Scout and Chief families.

The world's fastest Indian

Burt Munro and his 1920 Indian Scout

New Zealand racer Burt Munro spent decades developing his 1920 Indian Scout, transforming it into a highly modified speed machine. His work culminated in record-setting runs at Utah's Bonneville Salt Flats, including his celebrated 1967 record in the under-1,000 cc class.

His achievement became widely known through the 2005 film The World's Fastest Indian, starring Anthony Hopkins. The film tells a dramatized version of Munro's journey; historical records and the film are not interchangeable sources.

The original Scout, Munro's extensively modified racer and today's Scout models should each be described as distinct machines, even though they share the Indian name.
