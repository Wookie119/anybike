Generation and identification guide

Original 2002 VRSCA

The first VRSCA combined a liquid-cooled 1,130 cc Revolution V-twin with a distinctive aluminium-finish appearance. Later VRSC versions changed styling, equipment and sometimes engine capacity.

From VR1000 racing to the Revolution engine

Harley-Davidson's VR1000 road-racing programme informed the development of the V-Rod's Revolution engine, with Porsche Engineering involved in the powertrain programme. The VRSCA debuted as a 2002 model, using a liquid-cooled, 60-degree, double-overhead-cam V-twin rather than the traditional air-cooled Big Twin.

Different V-Rod models and generations

Early VRSCA motorcycles used a 1,130 cc Revolution engine. Later VRSC models introduced a larger 1,250 cc engine and included Night Rod, Night Rod Special, Street Rod and other variants. Don't apply one model's tyres, frame geometry, exhaust or suspension specifications to the whole family.

Why the V-Rod matters

The V-Rod connected Harley-Davidson's sporting and drag-racing ambitions to a distinctive factory power cruiser. It also supported the Screamin' Eagle Pro Stock Motorcycle drag-racing programme, another important strand of Harley-Davidson motorsport history.

What a UK buyer should check

Verify the exact VRSC variant and year, VIN, cooling system, electrics, fuel system, original exhaust, corrosion, keys and full service history. Check the specific motorcycle's market homologation and modifications before export.

V-Rod engine and generation reference

The original 2002 VRSCA used the liquid-cooled 1,131 cc Revolution V-twin with overhead camshafts. Later VRSC models include 1,250 cc versions: an engine-size distinction that matters when comparing specifications, parts and asking prices. Harley-Davidson's own model-code tables distinguish VRSCAW, VRSCD Night Rod, VRSCDX Night Rod Special and other versions.

  • Before buying: identify the model code, model year, engine displacement and market specification from the VIN and documents, rather than assuming that every V-Rod is the same.
  • Condition inspection: check cooling-system history, leaks, drivetrain condition, suspension wear and evidence of non-standard wiring or tuning. Request records for replacement parts and modifications.
  • Originality: compare the tank covers, wheels, forks, exhaust and other equipment against period photographs of the exact VRSC variant. A modified Night Rod must not be advertised as an original 2002 VRSCA.
