# AnyBike Procedure Manual
## Version 1.0 — Development Draft
**Status:** DEVELOPMENT / TRAINING ONLY  
**Purpose:** Preserve how AnyBike is intended to operate so that family and trusted staff can understand the business, continue running it safely, and improve it without losing the reasons behind key controls.

---

# 1. How to Use This Manual

This manual is not a static rulebook. It is the operating explanation behind the AnyBike platform.

Every important process should answer five questions:

1. **What are we trying to achieve?**
2. **Why do we do it this way?**
3. **Who owns the next action?**
4. **What proves the step is complete?**
5. **What happens next?**

AnyBike uses the same principle on the website:

> **Your next action**

The customer should always know what AnyBike needs from them next.  
The salesperson should always know what they need to do next.  
Operations, Accounts and drivers should each have a clear next action appropriate to their role.

Where a process does not fit a genuine commercial situation, staff may use an approved exception where the system allows it, but the reason must be recorded.

# 2. Development, Training and Live Trading

AnyBike is still being built. The process-learning system must never confuse website development with genuine business activity.

There are three operating environments:

## DEVELOPMENT
Used while pages, buttons, fields, calculations and workflows are being built or repaired.

Development activity:
- may be recorded for engineering history;
- must **not** be treated as evidence of how AnyBike really operates;
- must **not** generate process-learning recommendations.

This is the normal mode while the platform is still being constructed.

## TRAINING
Used for deliberate, realistic end-to-end rehearsals of the intended business process.

Training activity:
- may be analysed by the Process Advisor;
- is useful for identifying missing steps, confusing next actions and process gaps;
- is still not treated as actual commercial trading.

A test Deal should only be treated as TRAINING when staff are intentionally following the process as if it were a real sale.

## LIVE
Used only for real customers and real transactions.

Live evidence carries the greatest importance when deciding whether a process is working.

The AI / Process Advisor may:
- observe;
- detect patterns;
- identify delays;
- identify repeated overrides;
- identify repeated manual work;
- suggest process improvements.

It must **not** silently change business rules.

Human approval is always required before a suggested process change becomes part of the official procedure.

# 3. Core AnyBike Operating Principles

## 3.1 One clear next action
At any point in a Deal, there should be one obvious next action for the person responsible.

The system should not force staff or customers to understand the whole process before they can continue.

## 3.2 Customer money and supplier money are separate
Customer receipts do not automatically mean a motorcycle is secured.

Supplier payment does not automatically mean the customer has accepted the motorcycle.

Every movement of money must have a clear purpose, reference and Deal allocation.

## 3.3 Customer acceptance and seller security are separate
A customer accepting a Formal Offer is a customer decision.

A seller agreeing to sell, reserving the bike or being paid is a supplier-side event.

The system must never treat one as proof of the other.

## 3.4 Seller identity is protected
For sourced motorcycles, seller/dealer identity and source URLs remain internal unless there is a specific reason to disclose them.

The buyer sees the motorcycle presented by AnyBike.

## 3.5 Do not buy a motorcycle without a realistic onward route
Before AnyBike commits substantial money to a motorcycle, it should understand how the buyer expects the motorcycle to be handed over in the UK and shipped onward.

Saved shipper details are helpful, but the route must still be viable for the specific Deal.

## 3.6 Overrides are allowed when commercially sensible
AnyBike should not be so rigid that it prevents good business.

Examples include:
- established customers;
- trusted repeat buyers;
- checks genuinely not relevant to a particular customer;
- alternative shipping arrangements;
- strategic commercial decisions.

The reason for the override must be recorded.

## 3.7 Never hide a problem by changing a status
Statuses should describe reality.

Examples:
- a motorcycle is not “Secured” merely because staff want to move to the next page;
- a payment is not “Received” because a screenshot was sent;
- a shipper is not “Verified” because the buyer typed a company name;
- a Deal is not “Complete” because staff have finished their own work if the transaction itself is not complete.

# 4. Buyer Onboarding and Purchase Clearance

## Purpose
Create enough trust and operational certainty to buy motorcycles for a customer without making genuine buyers go through unnecessary bureaucracy.

## Why
AnyBike may commit significant money to motorcycles before they are exported. We need confidence in:
- who the buyer is;
- how they intend to pay;
- where the motorcycle will go;
- whether their shipper can receive it;
- whether there are obvious fraud or sanctions concerns.

## Standard buyer setup
Collect where appropriate:
- legal name;
- business name;
- email;
- telephone;
- address;
- proof of address;
- photo ID / passport;
- driving licence if useful;
- EORI or equivalent customs ID where relevant;
- destination country;
- destination port;
- preferred shipper / freight forwarder;
- shipper contact name;
- shipper email;
- shipper telephone;
- UK handover point / collection address;
- normal shipping method;
- normal shipment quantity.

Shipping method may include:
- FCL — Full Container Load;
- shared / consolidated container;
- LCL — Less than Container Load;
- RoRo;
- Air Freight;
- Other / Varies.

## Test payment
For a new or higher-risk buyer, AnyBike may request a small test payment.

The purpose is to help verify the paying account and payment relationship.

A small payment is **not** proof of identity by itself.

Staff must verify money actually received in the bank. A screenshot is not sufficient.

## Buyer approval outcomes
- Not Reviewed
- In Progress
- Approved
- Approved with Exceptions
- Review Required
- Blocked

Individual checks can be:
- Not Provided
- Provided
- Verified
- Not Required
- Waived
- Review Required
- Failed

## Approved with Exceptions
Use when the buyer is commercially acceptable but one or more checks are not relevant or are safely being waived.

The reason must be recorded.

## Account control
A buyer account may be:
- Active
- On Hold
- Stopped

A hold or stop must include a clear customer-facing reason.

The customer should be notified and shown a clear **Your next action** explaining what they need to do.

Stopped / On Hold buyers must not be able to accept a Formal Offer until the issue is resolved.

# 6. Sourcing Motorcycles

## Purpose
Find suitable motorcycles for the buyer without requiring AnyBike to own stock first.

## Why
AnyBike is a sourcing-led export business. A Deal represents the buyer requirement; candidate motorcycles sit underneath that Deal.

## Candidate motorcycle
A candidate may be:
- existing AnyBike stock;
- sourced manually;
- found through the Used Bike Scanner;
- found through a dealer;
- found through another approved source.

Internal information may include:
- seller / dealer;
- source URL;
- seller contact;
- registration;
- source stock number;
- internal asking price.

Buyer-safe information includes:
- make;
- model;
- variant;
- year;
- mileage;
- colour;
- description;
- AnyBike selling price;
- suitable images.

## Buyer response
The buyer may mark a motorcycle:
- Interested;
- Not Interested;
- Watching / equivalent where supported.

Buyer interest is not a reservation and is not a purchase.

# 7. Seller Availability

## Purpose
Confirm the motorcycle is genuinely available before AnyBike treats it as saleable.

## Why
Used motorcycles can sell quickly. Website adverts and dealer listings become stale.

## Procedure
1. Contact the seller.
2. Confirm the motorcycle is still available.
3. Confirm seller / business identity.
4. Confirm the current asking or agreed purchase price.
5. Record the live availability check.
6. Note any time-limited hold or reservation.

Before the motorcycle is secured, a customer Formal Offer must not remain valid beyond the seller-confirmed availability / hold window. If AnyBike has subsequently **genuinely secured the motorcycle**, that earlier availability window has completed its purpose and no longer limits the customer offer expiry.

If sold:
- mark candidate Sold / No Longer Available;
- keep the buyer's sourcing Deal open for alternatives.

# 8. Seller / Viability Review

## Purpose
Check that AnyBike can realistically buy and sell the motorcycle at an acceptable commercial position.

## Procedure
Review:
- seller price;
- negotiated purchase price;
- AnyBike Sourcing Fee (8% of Motorcycle Sale Price, rounded to nearest £5, minimum £395 unless deliberately overridden);
- delivery allowance;
- customer selling price;
- projected margin;
- VAT treatment;
- motorcycle availability;
- relevant seller notes.

## VAT
Use the correct VAT basis:
- VAT Qualifying;
- Margin Scheme;
- Not Yet Confirmed / Check Required.

VAT must be verified before purchase.

Do not expose confusing internal tax terminology unnecessarily to private buyers.

# 9. Formal Offer

## Purpose
Give the buyer one clear AnyBike offer with an auditable decision.

## Why
Customer acceptance must never be inferred from unrelated events.

## Procedure
1. Seller / Viability is complete.
2. Prepare Formal Offer.
3. Confirm motorcycle details.
4. Confirm the motorcycle price.
5. The AnyBike Sourcing Fee is calculated automatically at 8% of the Motorcycle Sale Price, rounded to the nearest £5, with a minimum fee of £395. Keep it separate from the motorcycle price. Staff may deliberately override the automatic fee for an exceptional commercial reason.
6. Add the UK Collection & Delivery Charge separately where applicable. Delivery does not increase or change the Sourcing Fee calculation.
7. Confirm the UK delivery / handover destination. Use the Deal or saved customer shipping preference first; otherwise choose from the maintained UK port list or enter the agreed UK address.
8. Confirm the motorcycle registration and/or VIN where known. Vehicle identity shown on a sent Formal Offer should be snapshotted so later edits do not silently change what the buyer accepted.
9. Confirm the resulting Customer Total.
10. Confirm delivery / payment terms where relevant.
11. Include the standard offer wording linking the Formal Offer to the current AnyBike Terms & Conditions and Trade & Export Sale Policy. The buyer still confirms the current legal declarations separately at acceptance.
12. Show the customer deposit and the remaining balance after that deposit so both sides can see the immediate payment obligation and what remains.
13. Send offer.
14. Sending a Formal Offer must automatically:
   - move the Deal to Proposal Sent;
   - create or link a Message Centre thread if one does not already exist;
   - send a customer Message Centre message;
   - create a customer notification linking to the offer;
   - make the offer visible in My AnyBike / My Offers;
   - preserve whether the motorcycle is already secured rather than incorrectly resetting or describing it as unsecured.
7. Customer reviews.
8. Customer explicitly Accepts or Declines.

A sent offer is not an accepted offer.

A motorcycle being secured from the seller is not customer acceptance.

Legacy offers that do not meet the current legal acceptance process must not be silently treated as valid current Formal Offers.

Perform a final common-sense check before AnyBike commits money to purchasing the motorcycle.

## Review

# 12. Securing the Motorcycle

## Purpose
Move from “we intend to buy it” to genuine control of the motorcycle.

## Procedure
Immediately before purchase:
1. Reconfirm availability.
2. Reconfirm agreed seller price.
3. Confirm seller identity / business.
4. Confirm any hold or reservation.
5. Confirm required seller deposit.
6. Confirm purchase terms.
7. Only then mark the motorcycle **Secured**.

## Meaning of Secured
“Secured” must describe a genuine commercial position.

It must not be used simply to unlock the next screen.

# 14. Customer Balance, Proforma and Documents

## Purpose
Make it impossible for a buyer to be asked for money against a document they have never received.

## Procedure
1. Create the correct Proforma.
2. If appropriate, combine multiple motorcycles onto one Proforma.
3. Send / expose the Proforma to the customer.
4. Give a clear payment amount and reference.

6. Record payment.

# 17. Buyer Shipper Authorisation and UK Handover

## Purpose
Hand the motorcycle only to a recipient authorised by the buyer.

## Buyer shipping profile
Reusable buyer details may include:
- shipper / freight forwarder;
- UK handover address or port;
- destination country;
- destination port;
- contact details;
- account / booking / quote reference;
- shipping method.

Where practical, the customer should choose the UK port / handover point and freight forwarder from controlled dropdown lists rather than retyping names. This reduces spelling errors and helps AnyBike reuse the same shipping data consistently. "Other / not listed" must remain available for genuine exceptions.

Saved details are defaults only.

If a Deal reaches the Formal Offer stage and the shipper is not yet known, staff may send a **Confirm your shipper / freight forwarder** request from the Formal Offer. The customer receives a Message Centre message and bell notification linking directly to Saved Shipping Preferences.

Each Deal must still confirm that the selected shipper / route can receive the motorcycle before AnyBike commits to purchase where the shipping route is required.

## Authorisation
The buyer can authorise a shipper to receive the motorcycle.

The shipper may receive an email confirming the buyer name and handover request.

## Delivery evidence
At final UK handover:
- capture delivery evidence;
- record recipient;
- record completion;
- shipper signage may be visible in handover evidence where useful.

# 20. Account Holds and Stops

## Use when
There is a genuine unresolved issue that means the transaction should not continue normally.

Examples:
- identity concern;
- payment issue;
- unresolved shipper problem;
- fraud concern;
- required information repeatedly missing;
- management decision.

## Procedure
1. Choose On Hold or Stopped.
2. Record the reason.
3. Tell the customer clearly.
4. Set the customer's **Your next action**.
5. Prevent inappropriate progression.
6. Resolve the underlying issue.
7. Reactivate when safe.
8. Notify the customer that the account can continue.

A stop is a control, not a punishment.

# 21. Exceptions and Overrides

AnyBike should remain commercially practical.

An exception is valid when:
- normal process does not fit the genuine situation;
- risk remains understood and acceptable;
- appropriate staff have authority;
- the reason is documented.

Examples:
- known repeat customer does not need a new test payment;
- shipper changes for one Deal;
- a check is Not Required for the buyer type;
- management accepts a lower-margin transaction.

Repeated overrides are important learning evidence.

If the same override happens repeatedly, the procedure may be wrong and should be reviewed.

# 28. First Order, Seller Purchase and Consolidation Rules — 1 October 2026

## Seller availability is not motorcycle ownership
A seller availability check records:
- the motorcycle is still available;
- the agreed seller purchase price;
- any temporary seller hold / reservation;
- the time until which that seller confirmation is valid.

A temporary hold does **not** mean the motorcycle is purchased or secured by AnyBike.

The customer Formal Offer has its own expiry. It does not need to end at exactly the same time as a temporary seller hold. AnyBike must recheck the seller immediately before paying the seller. If the saved seller availability has expired, supplier payment is blocked until the seller is rechecked.

## When AnyBike purchases the motorcycle
AnyBike only pays the seller after:
1. the buyer has accepted the Formal Offer;
2. AnyBike has received and allocated the buyer's full cleared payment for the Deal;
3. the buyer account is not On Hold or Stopped;
4. Buyer Verification and Export & Tax Identity are complete, unless the buyer is inside the controlled first-order seven-day grace period;
5. for an export/international transaction, the UK handover route is Confirmed;
6. the buyer's freight forwarder is recorded;
7. the UK shipping / receiving company is recorded separately where it is a different business;
8. the exact UK delivery / handover address and postcode are recorded;
9. seller availability has been rechecked and is still valid.

The motorcycle becomes **Purchased / Secured** when the seller purchase price has actually been paid in full. Staff should not mark a motorcycle secured merely because it is available or held.

## First-order seven-day verification grace
The first accepted Deal 360 Formal Offer for a new buyer starts one seven-day first-order grace period.

During this period the buyer may complete:
- Buyer Verification; and
- Export & Tax Identity.

The grace period does not remove the requirements for:
- cleared customer funds before AnyBike pays the seller;
- an active customer account;
- a current seller recheck; or
- a complete UK handover route for an export purchase.

Accepting additional motorcycles during the first order does not restart the Buyer Verification deadline.

## Freight forwarder versus UK receiving company
Do not assume the buyer's freight forwarder is the same business that receives the motorcycle in the UK.

Record separately:
- Buyer Freight Forwarder;
- UK Shipping / Receiving Company;
- UK receiver contact;
- exact UK delivery / handover address;
- postcode;
- port / terminal or handover-point label where relevant;
- booking / forwarder reference where available.

Move Motorcycles must know the exact UK delivery location before AnyBike pays the seller on an export Deal.

## First-order multi-bike consolidation and storage
A buyer's first order may contain several motorcycles.

When the first motorcycle in that first order enters depot storage:
- a seven-day free first-order consolidation period begins;
- further motorcycles can be added to the same first order;
- if the buyer genuinely commits to another motorcycle while the consolidation window is active, the consolidation deadline may extend to allow that committed motorcycle to join the shipment;
- merely saying they may buy another motorcycle is not a commitment.

If the buyer does not commit to another motorcycle or complete an agreed onward handover by the end of the free consolidation period, normal depot storage charges begin.

Current standard depot storage rate:
- **£6.95 per motorcycle per day including VAT.**

The operating objective remains to move paid-for motorcycles to the confirmed UK receiver promptly. Storage is for genuine consolidation or unavoidable delay, not a default holding stage.

Freight method, container size and international carrier choice remain transaction-specific and are not hard-coded into Deal 360.

# 29. Motorcycle Commercial Margin and Transport Treatment — 1 October 2026

## Purpose
Deal 360 must keep the motorcycle commercial decision separate from the customer transport line so staff do not create false profit or false costs.

## Customer Total
Customer Total is:
- Motorcycle Sale Price;
- plus AnyBike Sourcing Fee;
- plus UK Collection & Delivery Charge;
- plus any other separately agreed customer-facing charge where the workflow explicitly supports it.

The UK Collection & Delivery Charge remains a real customer charge. It is not removed from the offer or invoice merely because it is excluded from the motorcycle margin.

## Motorcycle commercial revenue
For the Deal 360 commercial margin calculation:

**Motorcycle commercial revenue = Motorcycle Sale Price + AnyBike Sourcing Fee**

Do not add the UK Collection & Delivery Charge to motorcycle commercial revenue.

## Motorcycle / Deal Cost Basis
Include only genuine motorcycle/deal-specific costs:
- Agreed Seller Purchase Price;
- genuine prep / rectification;
- genuine export documentation cost;
- genuine additional deal administration cost;
- genuine other deal-specific costs.

Do **not** invent an internal Move Motorcycles collection/delivery cost simply because AnyBike charges the buyer for transport. If a genuine accounted transport supplier cost or invoice exists in a future transaction, it must be handled according to the accounting design in force at that time rather than inserted as an assumed figure.

## Motorcycle commercial profit and margin
Calculate:

**Motorcycle Commercial Profit = Motorcycle commercial revenue - Motorcycle / Deal Cost Basis**

**Motorcycle Commercial Margin = Motorcycle Commercial Profit / Motorcycle commercial revenue**

Delivery must not increase either figure.

For AB-000026, with:
- Motorcycle Sale Price £13,835;
- AnyBike Sourcing Fee £1,105;
- UK Collection & Delivery Charge £250;
- Agreed Seller Purchase Price £12,000;
- no other genuine motorcycle/deal costs;

the operating figures are:
