# Indian historical motorcycle research — publication rules

## Scope
The original Springfield-era Indian Scout, Chief and Four are **historical research families**, not aliases for modern Indian Motorcycle stock. Their canonical editorial source is `data/indian-heritage-research.json`. The modern approved catalogue remains `data/motorcycle-index-identities.json`.

## Workflow
1. Identify the exact historic family, model year, generation and factory identity. Do not infer these from a modern dealer feed or the word “Scout” or “Chief” alone.
2. Attach dated factory literature, museum collections, period manuals or other attributable evidence for each technical claim. Record the source URL or archival citation and the exact year/variant it supports.
3. Separate factory-original specifications from racing modifications, restorations, aftermarket conversions and later reproductions. Burt Munro's modified Scout is not a factory-specification reference.
4. Obtain explicit rights or a suitable licence for each photograph and verify that its pictured model/year matches the record. No automatic image scraping.
5. Review each proposed generation before adding it to the shared model-guide research system. Keep the BMW R 1300 GS-approved design, universal sections and role-independent display rules.
6. Publish only facts that passed review. Leave unavailable sections unverified; do not fabricate technical specifications or stock availability.
7. Check links from the Indian manufacturer page, relevant AnyBike Global stories, sitemap and canonical URL. Check UK/international and logged-in/logged-out views.

## Publishing gates
Do not set historical records to approved stock, verified specification or rights-cleared photography without documented evidence and a review trail. Never add a competing hardcoded motorcycle model-page template.
