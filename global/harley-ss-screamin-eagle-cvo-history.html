S&S Cycle, Screamin’ Eagle and CVO: three different Harley-Davidson stories

An independent engine builder, a factory performance-parts programme and a low-volume factory motorcycle division all helped shape the Harley-Davidson performance world. They are not interchangeable labels.

S&S Cycle: the independent V-twin tradition

S&S began in Blue Island, Illinois, in 1958, with roots in independent efforts to make V-twin engines faster. Its company account records the move to Viola, Wisconsin, in 1969. S&S developed a reputation for performance parts, engines and independent custom-builder projects, distinct from Harley-Davidson's factory range.

S&S has also organised custom-bike builder showcases, including engine-centred build programmes. These events show the influence of independent workshops on American V-twin culture without implying that all participating motorcycles were factory Harley-Davidsons.

Screamin’ Eagle: Harley-Davidson's own performance range

Screamin’ Eagle is a Harley-Davidson factory performance-parts brand. Stage I focuses on intake, exhaust and calibration; subsequent stages can involve camshafts, displacement and cylinder-head changes. The steps differ by exact engine family, kit and model year.

Harley-Davidson explicitly warns that some Screamin’ Eagle products are not road-legal in all countries. A genuine Harley-Davidson accessory is therefore not automatic proof of UK road legality or insurance acceptance.

CVO: what makes a factory special edition?

Harley-Davidson launched Custom Vehicle Operation (CVO) in 1999 with FXR2 and FXR3 models limited to 900 units each. These were distinctive, premium factory-built motorcycles rather than simply standard bikes with bolt-on parts.

Harley-Davidson says most early CVO motorcycles carried Screamin’ Eagle names through 2008, with CVO branding taking over in 2009. CVO has since spanned different touring and adventure models; the exact edition, engine and equipment must be verified by year.

Why the differences matter when buying

Consider three motorcycles: one fitted with S&S parts, one upgraded with genuine Screamin’ Eagle parts, and an original factory CVO. They may all be unusual or desirable, but only the third is necessarily a factory CVO.

For a UK purchase, check the VIN and original model code, engine numbers if applicable, modification receipts, workshop documentation, original parts, insurance declarations, MOT and relevant emissions or noise requirements. Never rely on aftermarket badges alone for provenance.
