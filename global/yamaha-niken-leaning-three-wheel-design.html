Yamaha NIKEN: Why Two Front Wheels Change the Design

How Yamaha developed the NIKEN's leaning multi-wheel front end, and why it differs from a conventional motorcycle.

At first glance the Yamaha NIKEN looks like a machine from a design studio rather than a familiar road motorcycle. Its most distinctive feature is its pair of front wheels: both lean as the motorcycle corners, using Yamaha's Leaning Multi-Wheel technology. It is not a conventional rigid three-wheeler.

Yamaha introduced the NIKEN in 2017 as a sport-oriented extension of technology it had already developed for its Tricity models. A mechanical front-end arrangement allows the two front wheels to lean together. Yamaha also designed the steering geometry to retain a motorcycle-like response.

The additional front contact patch is intended to increase confidence and grip at the front, although it does not eliminate the need for proper tyres, braking judgment or riding skill. The NIKEN illustrates how changing the architecture of a motorcycle can affect both engineering and visual identity.

For a buyer comparing the NIKEN with a conventional Yamaha MT-series motorcycle, the right questions are about front-end feel, weight, maintenance, fit and intended riding—not simply the number of wheels.
