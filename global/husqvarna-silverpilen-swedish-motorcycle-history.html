Husqvarna’s Silverpilen: The Swedish motorcycle that helped shape the brand’s off-road identity

With a 175 cc two-stroke and a weight of 75 kg listed in its original brochure, the 1955 Silverpilen shows how far Husqvarna could go with a light, simple motorcycle.

28 November 2026 · AnyBike Global editorial

A light Swedish motorcycle with sporting ambitions

Husqvarna began producing motorcycles in Sweden in 1903 with a motorised bicycle. More than half a century later, the Silverpilen, Swedish for “Silver Arrow”, brought a different idea to the road. Its polished alloy mudguards gave it a distinctive look, while its light construction made it easy to see the appeal for riders who also wanted to compete.

The original brochure lists a three-speed gearbox, a tubular frame, a seven-litre steel fuel tank and 19-inch wheels at both ends. It also lists a headlamp, horn and speedometer. Those details put the Silverpilen in its everyday context alongside its sporting reputation.

From road riding to motocross

Husqvarna’s account connects the Silverpilen to the early careers of future motocross world champions Torsten Hallman and Rolf Tibblin. In a separate factory interview, Swedish racer Bengt Olov Wessman recalls arriving at races on a 175 cc Silverpilen in autumn 1955, when it was hardly an obvious choice for competition.

A lightweight bike gives a rider less mass to manage when changing direction or recovering from a mistake on rough ground. That is a useful way to understand the Silverpilen’s appeal, and why Husqvarna’s early off-road story can be told through a modest 175.
