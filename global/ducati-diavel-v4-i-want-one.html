Ducati Diavel V4: part cruiser, part muscle bike, all attitude

A low-slung silhouette, a V4 engine and the sort of rear tyre that starts conversations.

The Ducati Diavel V4 looks like a cruiser that has spent too much time around superbikes—in the best possible way. Its long, low stance and muscular proportions say one thing, while its engine and chassis engineering suggest something rather more energetic.

Unlike traditional cruisers built around a large twin-cylinder engine, the Diavel V4 uses Ducati's V4 Granturismo engine. Ducati designed it for strong performance alongside road-going usability, making this a distinctive proposition for someone who wants presence without abandoning modern motorcycle technology.

Look closely at the details: the prominent rear wheel, the sculpted tank and the distinctive lighting are part of the design story. The Diavel is a good reminder that engineering is not only about numerical specifications. Designers also decide how a machine should feel before its engine is even started.

Of course, wanting one and choosing one are different things. Riding position, insurance, passenger needs, running costs and local market specifications are worth checking before making a decision. But as an example of a motorcycle with real visual identity, this one certainly gets attention.
