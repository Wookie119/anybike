Canada's Can-Am Pulse and Origin: Two Ways to Go Electric

Compare the Can-Am Pulse urban electric motorcycle with the Origin dual-sport, and the practical decisions that distinguish them.

When Can-Am returned to two-wheeled motorcycles, it chose two distinct electric models rather than a single all-purpose design. BRP announced the Pulse and Origin line-up in 2024 for the 2025 model year, drawing on Can-Am's earlier off-road heritage.

The Pulse is the more road- and city-oriented motorcycle. Its compact form and road equipment suit commuting and urban journeys. The Origin uses a dual-sport layout, with suspension and riding modes intended for a mix of tarmac and trails.

Electric motorcycles change the ownership conversation. Peak torque arrives differently from a combustion engine, while charging access, journey length, battery management and real-world range become especially important. The best choice depends on where a rider travels and whether dependable charging is available.

Manufacturer range figures are measured under particular conditions. They should not be treated as promises for fast motorway riding, cold weather or heavily loaded journeys. Comparing the Pulse and Origin fairly means examining the applicable model-year specifications and local charging infrastructure.
