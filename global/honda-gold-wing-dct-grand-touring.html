Honda Gold Wing DCT: Rethinking the Grand Tourer

Explore how Honda's seven-speed dual-clutch transmission fits into the Gold Wing's long-distance design.

The Honda Gold Wing has long approached motorcycling from a touring perspective: comfort, passenger accommodation and refinement matter as much as performance. Its horizontally opposed six-cylinder engine helps distinguish it from motorcycles built primarily for urban commuting or track riding.

Honda's 2018 redesign introduced a seven-speed dual-clutch transmission option for the Gold Wing Tour. Instead of a conventional clutch lever and foot-shift routine, the system automatically handles clutch engagement and gear changes while retaining rider-selected modes.

The engineering matters because a large touring motorcycle faces unusual demands. Smooth low-speed manoeuvring, load-carrying comfort and easy progress on long journeys all influence the choice of transmission and rider assistance.

DCT should not be confused with a scooter's continuously variable transmission. The Gold Wing's DCT operates a geared transmission using two clutches, while a typical scooter CVT varies its drive ratio through a different mechanism. Both reduce manual shifting effort, but their mechanical approaches are different.
